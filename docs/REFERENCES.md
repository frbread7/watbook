# Public technical references

**Links checked:** 2026-10-12. References are cited near the relevant chapters and model notes. A reference supports only the claims identified there; it is not blanket support for every textbook statement. WATBook summarizes ideas in original wording and does not reproduce protected figures, standards, tables, or vendor content.

1. **Joint Committee for Guides in Metrology (JCGM).** *Evaluation of measurement data — Guide to the expression of uncertainty in measurement*, JCGM 100:2008(E), and *Amendment 1: Nonlinearity in measurement models*, JCGM 100:2008/Amd.1:2026. [BIPM Guides in Metrology index](https://www.bipm.org/en/web/guest/publications/guides). The index identifies the 2026 amendment specifically as addressing nonlinearity in measurement models. Consult the current guide and amendment for formal use; WATBook's budget simulator uses a clearly stated independent-components RSS teaching example.
2. **Taylor, B. N., and Kuyatt, C. E.** *Guidelines for Evaluating and Expressing the Uncertainty of NIST Measurement Results*, NIST Technical Note 1297, 1994 edition (current NIST page updated 2026-05-06). [NIST publication page](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297). Supports Type A/Type B evaluation, combined and expanded uncertainty, propagation, and reporting. It is metrology guidance, not a semiconductor test recipe.
3. **NIST/SEMATECH.** *e-Handbook of Statistical Methods*, Chapter 2, Measurement Process Characterization. [Handbook](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/mpc.htm). Covers measurement-process characterization, check standards, gauge studies, bias/variability, and uncertainty analysis.
4. **Tektronix/Keithley.** *Low Level Measurements Handbook*, 7th edition. [Public handbook PDF](https://download.tek.com/document/LowLevelHandbook_7Ed.pdf). Practical background on low-level current/voltage measurement, guarding, shielding, settling, and source-measure methods. Instrument-specific advice must be checked against the chosen instrument's current manual and configuration.
5. **NIST/SEMATECH.** *e-Handbook of Statistical Methods*, Chapter 6, Process or Product Monitoring and Control: [control charts](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc31.htm), [EWMA](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc314.htm), [CUSUM](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc323.htm), and [process capability](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/ppc/section4/ppc46.htm). These are links for statistical background; WATBook does not implement a general SPC or drift-detection simulator. It focuses on interpreting existing control evidence for parametric decisions.
6. **UC Berkeley BSIM Group.** [BSIM4 model page](https://bsim.berkeley.edu/models/bsim4/). The page lists BSIM4 4.8.3 Standard, released 2025-05-19, with code, benchmark tests, technical manual, and update document. WATBook's curve generator is not BSIM4 and is not calibrated to measured devices.
7. **Ortiz-Conde, A., et al.** “A review of recent MOSFET threshold voltage extraction methods.” *Microelectronics Reliability*, 42(4–5), 583–596 (2002). [DOI: 10.1016/S0026-2714(02)00027-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0026-2714(02)00027-6). Reviews the dependence of extracted threshold values on method and operating regime. WATBook uses this only to motivate declaring an extraction convention.
8. **Xu, H., Zhang, J., Lv, Y., and Zheng, P.** “Hybrid Feature Selection for Wafer Acceptance Test Parameters in Semiconductor Manufacturing.” *IEEE Access*, 8, 17320–17330 (2020). [DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2020.2966520](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2020.2966520). A case study of WAT-parameter selection for yield analysis; its dataset and fitted relationships are not universal process limits or causal laws.
9. **“Semiconductor Parametric Yield Prediction: A Combination of Machine Learning and Physics-Informed Approach.”** *2025 Annual Reliability and Maintainability Symposium (RAMS)*. [DOI: 10.1109/RAMS48127.2025.10935067](https://doi.org/10.1109/RAMS48127.2025.10935067). Discusses parametric-yield modeling from manufacturing test evidence. WATBook's synthetic association lab is not a reproduction of its dataset or a substitute for an independently validated predictive model.
10. **Hansen, M. H., Nair, V. N., and Friedman, D. J.** “Monitoring Wafer Map Data From Integrated Circuit Fabrication Processes for Spatially Clustered Defects.” *Technometrics*, 39(3), 241–253 (1997). [DOI: 10.1080/00401706.1997.10485116](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.1997.10485116). Supports the use of wafer-level spatial information in monitoring; WATBook's illustrative map generator does not implement this paper's clustering statistics.
11. **SEMI.** “Major Revision Underway for SEMI E142,” *Standards Watch* (2026-04). [Public article](https://www.semi.org/en/standards-watch-2026-apr/major-revision-underway-for-semi-e142). Notes that test/prober wafer coordinates may not match the E142 substrate-map coordinate system and that alignment matters for tracking. This article is not the normative standard; no SEMI standard text is reproduced.
12. **Oussalah, S., Djezzar, B., and Jerisian, R.** “A comparative study of different contact resistance test structures dedicated to the power process technology.” *Solid-State Electronics*, 49(10), 1617–1622 (2005). [Publisher record and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sse.2005.08.004). Compares TLM, cross-bridge Kelvin resistor, and contact-end-resistance structures. WATBook links this as further reading only; it does not implement or teach a TLM extraction lab.

## Synthetic data and provenance

All values produced by WATBook's interactive laboratories are generated locally from documented deterministic formulae and seeds. They are **synthetic teaching data**, not foundry measurements, open production datasets, or samples extracted from cited papers. The labs do not send data to a server.
