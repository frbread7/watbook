Respect the nesting

A convenient starting model for replicate r measured at site k on wafer j in lot i is
yijkr = μ + Li + Wij + Sijk + Mijkr + εijkr, where L denotes lot-level, W wafer-within-lot, S spatial, M measurement-context, and ε residual contributions. The replicate index r identifies repeated readings of the same site. The terms are not automatically independent, Gaussian, or separately identifiable. The equation is a bookkeeping model: it forces the analyst to ask which design and metadata distinguish each effect.

Repeated points across a wafer inform within-wafer pattern; repeated wafers across lots inform lot and wafer variation. Repeated measurements on the same site under intentionally changed channel or order can help estimate measurement-context terms. If every center site was measured on channel 1 and every edge site on channel 2, spatial and channel effects are confounded. No regression can recover the two effects from that design without additional assumptions or new data.

Spatial descriptions need coordinate provenance

Preserve die or site coordinate, units, origin, axis orientation, notch orientation, exclusion mask, and the transform to every downstream coordinate system. A pattern can rotate or mirror when one system changes origin or handedness. Record both raw tester coordinates and any transformed coordinates; retain the transform definition and revision. Never compare “north edge” across exports until their orientation convention is established.

Spatial patterns may be represented using radial terms, smooth surfaces, spatial covariance, or cluster statistics. The right method depends on the site layout and the suspected length scale. An illustrative radial component S(x,y)=a·r²,   r=√(x²+y²),   r≤1 is used on an ideal circular domain, with radius normalized to its edge. The simulator samples that circle on a square lattice and omits points outside it. It does not model die geometry, a notch, or an edge-exclusion rule, and it is not a physical process law. Real wafer behavior may be anisotropic, piecewise, tool-specific, or dominated by edge exclusions. The Hansen–Nair–Friedman paper studies spatially clustered defect maps; WATBook does not implement its procedure.

Separate process structure from channel and order

The hierarchical map exercise generates known synthetic lot, wafer, radial, channel, sequence, and noise components. Sites inside the ideal circular domain are colored by measured value and positioned on the square lattice. Its expandable site table reports each site's coordinates and generated components; the initial map summary reports the site count, range, and mean. The circle is not a die map or an edge-exclusion specification. Change the seed to see how random variation changes without changing the model definition.

In the exercise, random noise is the sum of two independent uniform deviates centered at zero and scaled to a declared standard deviation. This creates bounded triangular noise, not Gaussian noise. The channel offsets cycle with site sequence, so they can appear spatial when measurement order follows row order. The known source decomposition is available only because the simulator generated it. For real data, use a deliberate channel swap, randomized order, reference site, or repeat measurement to challenge an artifact hypothesis.

Summaries do not replace maps

Mean, median, standard deviation, quantiles, and tail percentiles answer different questions. A mean can hide an edge ring; a range can be driven by one contact failure; a percentile may be unstable in a small sample. Report the number of lots, wafers, sites, missing sites, exclusions, and the method used to aggregate. Keep maps and hierarchy available alongside scalar summaries.

Spatial signal is not necessarily process causality. A pattern may track chamber, reticle, thermal gradient, wafer handling, test-channel arrangement, or coordinate conversion. Identify the comparison population and preserve tool/route/time context before ranking explanations.

Knowledge check

A radial-looking WAT map disappears when channel assignment is randomized and reappears by channel in a reference structure. What is the strongest conclusion? The pattern is associated with the measurement channel under the new experiment; the original map alone did not identify a radial process cause.
