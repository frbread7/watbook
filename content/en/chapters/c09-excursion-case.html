Case statement: a fictional excursion

Every number in this case is synthetic. A process integration team reports a 22 mV median increase in an extracted transistor threshold metric on three consecutive lots. A set of monitor sites appears more shifted near one side of the wafer. Product electrical die sort (EDS) has not yet shown a consistent lot-level change. The test program was revised shortly before the first affected lot, and the prober channel map was also updated.

The task is not to guess which change caused the signal. First define the metric and comparison population; preserve raw traces and revisions; check whether the map’s coordinate transform and channel assignment changed; establish the measurement uncertainty and repeatability; then choose discriminating measurements. Do not change a product disposition limit or imply a release decision from this synthetic case.

Evidence ledger

  1. Observation: median extracted metric changed by 22 mV; the baseline and new-program values must be reprocessed under a common extraction convention.
  2. Confounder: program revision may change the sweep, normalization, or fit interval.
  3. Confounder: channel map and coordinate transform changed; apparent spatial structure may be a mapping artifact.
  4. Independent evidence: EDS is presently inconclusive; its product population and power are not enough to rule out a monitor shift.
  5. Unknown: no device or inline measurement yet separates process change from measurement change.

This ledger keeps observed facts, metadata changes, and hypotheses in separate columns. An absent signal is informative only relative to the measurement’s sensitivity and sampling; “EDS did not move” is not automatically evidence of no process change.

Choose the next experiment

A practical sequence is: (1) freeze the current data and establish traceability; (2) compare old and new extraction on compatible raw curves; (3) rerun reference sites with crossed channel assignments and verified coordinates; (4) measure independent structures that should respond differently to leading device hypotheses; (5) review actual process records and inline data; and (6) collect appropriately grouped downstream test data. Predeclare what each result would support or contradict.

Do not select the next experiment solely because it is easy to collect. Choose one with a good chance of separating the leading candidates and a clear decision consequence. If the measurement system itself is suspect, repair that evidence before interpreting new process data.

Competing hypotheses

The interactive case on the lab page displays candidate hypotheses and observations. The score is only transparent ordinal bookkeeping. Selecting a stable reference that also shifts on the suspect channel supports a possible channel contribution, but does not prove it. A different result—an unchanged reference on the crossed channel while the target shift persists—would weigh against a simple channel offset; that counter-observation is not a checkbox in this small exercise.

A realistic engineering review should record analyst, data extract revision, criteria for exclusions, counterevidence, follow-up owner, and the time at which the decision was made. Keep the conclusion at the level justified by evidence: “the result is consistent with a test-channel contribution” is different from “the process is good.”

Knowledge check

What additional evidence would most directly challenge an apparent channel artifact? Interleave a stable reference and repeated monitor measurements while deliberately swapping channel assignment, then compare the response with the original map under a validated coordinate transform.
