Design monitor coverage and sampling around a falsifiable engineering question.

Begin with the decision and failure mode

Monitor design starts with a decision, not with a list of structures that happens to exist. Write down the process or product question, the population at risk, the response time required, and the consequence of a missed or false signal. Then derive the observables that could discriminate plausible explanations. A monitor is useful when its geometry, sensitivity, sampling, and test conditions make the needed distinction measurable.

For each proposed monitor, document the target measurand, structure family, expected response mechanism, relevant process modules, known non-target sensitivities, required test range, site placement, sample cadence, and downstream decision. Include a countermeasure: a reference structure, alternate geometry, independent measurement, or process split that can challenge the leading interpretation.

Structures are sensors with transfer functions

A MOS capacitor, transistor, diode, resistor, contact chain, or comb structure converts a physical state into an electrical response. That response depends on dimensions, contacts, parasitics, material state, temperature, stress, and the measurement circuit. A structure’s label is not a complete measurand. “Contact resistance,” for example, requires a named structure and extraction equation; different geometries can include different portions of sheet, spreading, and interface resistance.

Use structure diversity to separate competing effects only when the contrasts are designed. A long-channel and short-channel pair may react differently to short-channel effects, but only if layout, orientation, process context, and extraction are sufficiently comparable. Redundant structures can improve repeatability; orthogonal structures can improve diagnosis. The desired balance depends on the decision, area budget, test time, and monitor lifetime.

Sampling must match the hypothesis

Sampling is a design choice. Center-edge sites can probe radial patterns; orientation and azimuth can reveal directionality; replicated sites can assess local spatial and structure-to-structure variation; repeated measurements on the same stable site under an unchanged protocol are the relevant observations for short-term repeatability. Repeated wafers and lots are needed to understand higher-level variation. A sparse monitor grid may alias a narrow ring or edge excursion. A dense grid on one wafer still cannot estimate between-lot variation. Record why each site is selected and what spatial scale the design can resolve.

Test time and access constraints often force subsampling. A defensible plan states the inclusion rule, exclusions, number of independent lots and wafers, stratification variables, missing-site handling, and precision or detection target. If sites are selected because they already look abnormal, the selection rule must be recorded; a follow-up sample is not an unbiased estimate of the original population.

Coverage planner: what the score does not mean

The interactive planner on the WAT laboratory page maps selected illustrative monitor families to named measurands. Its coverage fraction is a bookkeeping aid: it does not validate a test structure, establish sensitivity, prove independence, or qualify a process. Use it to find omissions in a proposal, then make the engineering argument with geometry, physics, process context, and an explicit measurement plan.

Monitor menus change over time. Do not treat the sample structure-to-measurand mapping in the lab as a standard library or as a recommendation for a specific technology. The structures are generic educational examples and contain no foundry design rules.

Coordinate and traceability plan

At design release, define die/site identifiers, wafer orientation and notch convention, units, origin, handedness, reticle transforms, excluded regions, and how test coordinates map to process or downstream map systems. E142-style map exchange standards are context-specific; a public standards update notes that prober/test and substrate-map coordinates may not align without an explicit transform. Verify actual customer and system requirements rather than inferring a universal coordinate convention.

Knowledge check

A monitor observes only one site per wafer and 30 wafers from one lot. Can the design estimate within-wafer radial behavior? No. Increasing wafers improves lot-level information but does not replace spatial sampling within a wafer. The design must match the variation scale in the question.
