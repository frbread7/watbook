Declare the measurement contract

A production parameter is reproducible only if its measurement contract is reproducible. The contract names the physical structure, terminals and polarity, force and sense arrangement, stimulus waveform or sweep, range and compliance, dwell/settling rule, temperature and stabilization, contact sequence, extraction code and version, valid range, and data-quality flags. Instrument model and calibration state may also matter for traceability.

These fields are not paperwork added after a plot. They determine which measurand is observed. Changing drain bias during threshold extraction, for example, changes the operating condition and may change the extracted value. Changing current range or integration time may change noise and settling. The record needs enough information to reproduce the operation, not merely the final scalar.

Timing, leakage and compliance

A source-measure unit (SMU) applies a programmed voltage or current while measuring the complementary response. The displayed value after a source transition can contain instrument settling, cable and chuck charging, dielectric absorption, contact stabilization, and genuine device transients. A fixed delay is not automatically sufficient across structures and ranges. Use a characterized settling criterion, preserve the time trace when transient behavior matters, and state when the reported measurand is an equilibrium or timed response.

Current compliance limits the source response for protection; reaching it means the programmed voltage may not have been achieved. Range changes and autoranging can introduce transient behavior. Leakage paths in fixtures and probe cards can be comparable to a high-resistance measurement. Guarding, shielding, cable layout, and zero checks are practical parts of the measurement system, but their effectiveness must be demonstrated under the actual configuration.

Temperature includes more than a chuck set point. Stabilization, gradients, wafer self-heating, chuck contact, and elapsed time after load can affect the device and measurement. Record the controlled quantity and the evidence that it was stable enough for the chosen parameter. Do not compare datasets just because both metadata fields say “25 °C” if one is nominal set point and the other is verified wafer temperature.

Contact and sequence are experimental factors

Contact resistance, scrub history, probe marks, alignment, pad contamination, and tip wear can alter readings. Repeated touches can reduce one artifact while creating another. A channel assignment that follows wafer position can mimic a spatial process signature. Randomizing or rotating measurement order, swapping channels, using reference sites, and recording contact outcomes help distinguish these effects. The right diagnostic experiment depends on probe architecture and the electrical level being measured.

General prober and low-level measurement practice is outside WATBook’s scope; see the independent TestBook interface chapter and the Keithley Low Level Measurements Handbook. The WAT-specific requirement is to connect such conditions to each parameter’s extraction contract and to keep test-system evidence available during engineering interpretation.

Validity checks before interpretation

  • Confirm structure, pins, polarity, stimulus, range, compliance, and program revision.
  • Verify temperature and elapsed settling criteria; identify transitions and range changes.
  • Flag open/short/contact failures and out-of-range measurements instead of coercing them into plausible numbers.
  • Compare reference structures and repeat measurements under a declared protocol.
  • Keep raw traces and extracted values linked by stable record IDs.

Do not automatically delete an outlier because it is inconvenient. First determine whether it is a recording error, a measurement-system failure, or a real process signal. Keep the raw row, the decision rule, and the reason for exclusion in the analysis record.

Knowledge check

Two wafers differ by 6% in off-state leakage, but one run reached compliance and changed current range. Is the percentage a valid device comparison? Not until compliance and range behavior are resolved. A clipped response is not the intended leakage measurement.
