Communicate conclusions, limits, and follow-up evidence in a portable review record.

Write a portable WAT conclusion

An engineering handoff should let another qualified engineer reproduce the comparison and understand the limits. State the question and decision; structures and population; raw and extracted parameter definitions; test, extraction, and map revisions; sample hierarchy and exclusions; estimate and uncertainty; measurement-validity checks; analysis method; strongest supporting and contradicting evidence; and unresolved assumptions.

A compact conclusion may use this pattern: “For [defined population and interval], [named parameter] changed by [estimate] under [protocol], with [uncertainty and sample structure]. The change is [detectable / not resolved] relative to [baseline]. Evidence is consistent with [bounded hypothesis], but [alternatives] remain. [Named follow-up] can discriminate [predictions]. This statement does not establish [causality, product impact, or disposition].”

Check reproducibility before sharing

  • Run the committed tests, rebuild the site, and confirm both language editions use the same chapter sequence.
  • Verify all reported simulator URLs and anchors on the deployed revision.
  • Link raw data identifiers and analysis scripts without silently changing access controls.
  • Preserve license notices, citation details, and data provenance.
  • Record review scope and outstanding technical limitations.

WATBook’s tests validate its deterministic formulae, constraints, route generation, and browser journeys. They do not certify a test system, validate a semiconductor process, or substitute for laboratory data. The model-status table in References and glossary distinguishes mathematical checks from external reference checks.

Suggested progression

Start with Chapters 1–3 to define the decision and measurement contract. Use Chapters 4–6 when reviewing extraction or multi-level data. Chapters 7–9 support a structured investigation; Chapter 10 turns that work into a traceable handoff. The six lab widgets are small exercises: they do not form an automatic control or release system.

For a first-principles device treatment, follow DeviceBook. For general process control and yield, follow YieldBook; for product-specific BCD examples, follow PMICBook. The proposed My Library roadmap places advanced cross-module integration and operational fab control in separate future books.

Final review questions
  1. What exact measurement and population support the claim?
  2. Which potential artifacts were experimentally challenged?
  3. How were repeated sites, wafers, and lots treated?
  4. What observation would change the current interpretation?
  5. Which limitations remain before the result can inform a product decision?
